# Unslop Rules

The numbers are stable IDs. Other skills cite them. A removed rule leaves a gap. Rules 1, 2, 4, 6 and 21 do not exist.

Each rule changes only how a fact is said. No rule removes a fact or makes it weaker or stronger. SKILL.md defines a fact.

## Content

3. **Shallow -ing phrases.** "highlighting...", "ensuring...", "reflecting...", "showcasing...", "fostering...". Remove the phrase, or expand it with its real source or effect. Keep each fact in it.
5. **Vague attributions.** "Experts believe", "Industry reports suggest", "Some critics argue". Name the source when the text gives it. Otherwise keep the claim and its attribution.

## Language

7. **AI words.** Additionally, crucial, delve, enduring, enhance, fostering, garner, interplay, intricate, landscape (abstract), pivotal, showcase, tapestry (abstract), testament, underscore, vibrant. Use a plain word.
8. **Long forms of "is".** "serves as", "stands as", "boasts", "features". Write "is" or "has".
9. **"Not just X, but Y" and "not X, it is Y".** State the point directly. Keep X when X is a fact. Keep the negation when it is a fact.
10. **Rule of three.** Do not force items into groups of three. Use the real number of items.
11. **Synonym cycling.** Do not use many names for one thing. Use one name each time.
12. **False ranges.** "from X to Y" where X and Y are not on one scale. Name the topics directly.

## Style

13. **Em dashes.** Remove every em dash. Use a period, a comma, or parentheses. Keep each parenthesis that the text has.
14. **Colon as a connector.** Use a colon before a list or an example. Do not use a colon to join two clauses.
15. **Too much bold.** Do not make each name or acronym bold.
16. **Inline-header lists.** A bold label and a colon that repeat the line are a tell. Change them to prose. A bold lead-in is correct when it names the item, ends in a period, and comes before new detail.
17. **Title case headings.** Use sentence case.
18. **Decorative emojis.** Remove them from headings and bullets.
19. **Curly quotes.** Use straight quotes.

## Chat phrases

20. **Chatbot phrases.** "I hope this helps!", "Let me know if...", "Of course!", "Certainly!", "Found the smoking gun!". Remove them.
22. **Flattery.** "Great question!", "You're absolutely right!". Remove it. Answer directly.

## Filler

23. **Filler phrases.** "In order to" becomes "to". "Due to the fact that" becomes "because". Remove "It is important to note that".
24. **Too much hedging.** "could potentially possibly be argued that it might" becomes "may".
25. **Generic conclusions.** "The future looks bright." State a specific plan or fact, or remove the sentence.

## Jargon

26. **Abstract metaphor nouns.** Use the concrete word. Substrate (base), wedge or wedge in (add), vector (way, method). Primitive as a noun, harness as a metaphor, surface as in "API surface". Scaffolding as a metaphor, ratchet as a metaphor (a limit that only gets tighter). Evacuate for code that moves (move out), endgame (the last phase). Also locus, vantage, nexus, bedrock, modality, paradigm, north star, flywheel. Gold-plating (more than the job needs). A literal use of these words is correct.

## Plain speech

27. **Say what it does.** Name the mechanism or a number, not a feeling. If a sentence gives no fact, instruction or number, remove it. If a sentence fits any other project unchanged, remove it.
28. **Dense sentences.** Split a sentence that the reader must read twice. Use one idea in each sentence.
29. **Active voice.** Look for "is", "are", "was" or "were" before a past participle. Name the actor. "The loader parses the file" is correct. Use the passive only when the actor is not known or not important.
30. **Adverbs.** Remove the adverb, or use a stronger verb or the number.
31. **Plain words.** `utilize` and `leverage` become `use`. `facilitate` becomes `help`. `numerous` becomes `many`. `in the event that` becomes `if`.
32. **Mannered prose.** Replace each figure with a literal phrase. Figures are aphorisms, rhetorical fragments, personified code, figurative verbs, and stock framing phrases. Rule 26 covers the metaphor nouns.
33. **Over-compression.** Do not drop articles or verbs. Spell out arrows and abbreviations. Write full sentences.
